\begin{abstract}
Remote-sensing instance segmentation often involves similar or adjacent instances that are difficult to separate under shadows and narrow inter-instance gaps. Common proposal-box-driven methods provide insufficient shape cues for accurate instance-mask prediction.
To address this issue, we propose PromptMiner-SAM2, 
a two-stage segmentation framework that mines proposal-level shape cues and injects them into prompt-conditioned SAM2 mask decoding.
The coarse shape cues are first mined and serve as complementary instance-specific prompts for SAM2 mask prediction. 
To refine margin lines of instances, an uncertainty-guided decoder-tail module is designed to further correct uncertain local pixels. 
Experiments on the WHU and NWPU VHR-10 benchmarks show that PromptMiner-SAM2 obtains 73.4\% and 68.3\%
mask AP values, respectively, achieving the highest mask AP among the compared
methods.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
Instance segmentation, remote sensing imagery, SAM2, prompt mining, mask refinement
\end{keywords}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

Overhead instance segmentation supports cadastral mapping, urban planning, and post-disaster assessment~\cite{rsiisn},~\cite{p2pformer}. 
In dense overhead scenes, visually similar adjacent instances are difficult to separate under shadows, low contrast, and narrow inter-instance gaps. 
Once candidate instances have been localized, a rectangular proposal box provides only a coarse spatial extent and insufficient shape cues for accurate instance-mask prediction. 
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation}, box-only prompting can localize neighboring buildings but leaves their separating boundary under-specified, motivating richer instance-specific shape cues for mask prediction.

Existing remote-sensing instance segmentation methods couple instance discovery with mask prediction through two-stage, one-stage, cascade, or conditional designs~\citerange{maskrcnn}{condinst}. Transformer-style frameworks provide another modeling route by using global sequence representations for set-based prediction~\citerange{mask2former}{rs4d}. Alternative instance representations have also been explored through diffusion-based filter denoising and architecture search for efficient prediction~\citerange{diffusioninst}{efficientis}. These approaches generally rely on rectangular proposals or generic instance queries, which provide limited target-shape cues when neighboring objects have similar appearance. Beyond these architectures, prompt-conditioned foundation models offer a complementary way to inject spatial constraints into mask decoding. SAM and SAM2 accept sparse or dense prompts, motivating remote-sensing adaptation and automatic prompt learning such as RSPrompter~\citerange{sam}{pasam}. SAMRefiner constructs multiple prompt types from an initial mask, while PointRend predicts at uncertain locations~\cite{samrefiner},~\cite{pointrend}.

These lines of work point to a common opportunity: prompt-conditioned decoders can benefit from instance-specific spatial cues beyond a rectangular extent. In a proposal-driven SAM2 pipeline, the detector already produces proposal-level features, but a box alone does not fully convey the shape of the localized instance for mask prediction. Preserving and transforming these cues into prompt-compatible guidance therefore becomes important for connecting instance discovery with accurate SAM2 mask decoding. To this end, we propose PromptMiner-SAM2, a two-stage framework that integrates proposal-based instance discovery with prompt-conditioned SAM2 segmentation.

Specifically, our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose a proposal-driven two-stage framework for remote-sensing
  instance segmentation, coupling SAM2 with detector-based proposal extraction
  to connect instance discovery with prompt-conditioned mask decoding.
  \item We propose the coarse-shape prompt mining module (CSPM), which converts
  proposal-level shape estimates into complementary prompts to preserve
  instance-specific cues for separating adjacent objects.
  \item We design the uncertainty-guided decoder-tail refinement module (UDPR),
  which targets ambiguous mask locations with localized residual corrections to
  improve instance-mask delineation.
\end{itemize}

\section{METHOD}
As depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, PromptMiner-SAM2 couples a two-stage
detector with the SAM2 prompt-to-mask interface. Hiera features feed the
PAFPN/RPN/RoI pathway for proposal extraction, while the SAM2 mask decoder
combines the image embedding with proposal-conditioned prompts. The resulting
decoder features and mask logits are refined at uncertain locations, yielding
the final instance masks for each detected proposal after post-processing.

\subsection{Coarse-Shape Prompt Mining}
CSPM converts each proposal's RoI feature into a local shape estimate and uses it to construct complementary
geometric cues for SAM2. It outputs 2P2N point prompts, the proposal box, and a
dense shape prompt in the image-aligned coordinate frame.

\indent For each proposal, the coarse branch maps its RoI feature $X_r$ to a
proposal-local shape logit map
\begin{equation}
C_r=f_{\mathrm{coarse}}(X_r),\qquad C_r\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times64\times64}.
\label{eq:coarse}
\end{equation}
\indent Let $p_r=\sigma(C_r)$. PromptMiner derives a foreground support at probability
$0.5$ from $p_r$, while retaining the signed logits $C_r$ for the dense prompt.
It mines two positive and two negative points (2P2N) from this shared coarse
shape. The first positive point is selected among confident foreground interior
pixels ($p_r\geq0.6$) by its foreground confidence weighted by its normalized
distance to the support boundary; the second follows the same criterion while
remaining spatially separated from the first. The first negative point comes
from the one-pixel outer ring with low foreground probability ($p_r\leq0.3$).
The second is selected from background outside a dilation of the foreground
support and is separated from the first positive and ring-negative points.

\indent These four points encode complementary interior, exterior, and background
cues. The 2P2N set maps them to signed prompts in the image frame.

\indent Coordinates map to the image frame; signed logits are~resized and pasted into
$b_r$'s mask-input canvas:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_r=\mathcal{M}(C_r,b_r),\qquad
Q_r=\operatorname{Place}(C_r,b_r),
\label{eq:prompt-mining}
\end{equation}
\indent Here, $\mathcal{M}$ denotes 2P2N selection and coordinate mapping, and
$\operatorname{Place}$ is the resize-and-paste operation. The two corners of
$b_r$ provide the box prompt. SAM2 jointly encodes the point, box, and
dense-mask prompts, then decodes them with the image embedding in one pass:
\begin{equation}
Y_r^0=\mathcal{D}\bigl(\mathcal{E}_I(I),
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_P(\mathcal{S}_r,b_r,Q_r)\bigr),
\label{eq:native-decoding}
\end{equation}
\indent where $\mathcal{E}_I$, $\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_P$, and $\mathcal{D}$ denote
the SAM2 image encoder (including its decoder-side high-resolution features),
complete SAM2 prompt-embedding pathway, and mask decoder, respectively.
The pathway $\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_P$ includes the SAM2 composition of
the dense-mask embedding. $Q_r$ retains the signed rather than binarized
coarse logits. Thus, the dense prompt preserves the spatial variation of the
proposal-local shape, while the point and box prompts provide discrete
geometric constraints to the same decoder.

\subsection{Uncertainty-Guided Refinement}
UDPR focuses on ambiguous locations in the initial SAM2 mask. It combines local decoder
features with instance-level context to predict localized residual corrections.
The same decoding pass returns an upscaled decoder feature map $U_r$, the
first output mask token $t_r$, and decoder logit map $Y_r^0$.
On the raster grid $\mathcal{G}_r$, UDPR visits the $K_r$ locations with
the smallest logit magnitudes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K_r&=\min(K,|\mathcal{G}_r|),\\
\Omega_r&=\operatorname{BottomK}\bigl(|Y_r^0|,K_r\bigr),\qquad K=64,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:udpr-selection}
\end{equation}
\indent where $\operatorname{BottomK}$ returns the $K_r$ indices in $\mathcal{G}_r$
with the smallest logit magnitudes.
\indent For each $i\in\Omega_r$, the aligned feature $z_i$ is gathered from $U_r$ and
combined with the token, normalized location, local logit, and logit magnitude:
\begin{equation}
\phi_i=[z_i,t_r,Y^0_{r,i},x_i,y_i,|Y^0_{r,i}|].
\label{eq:udpr-input}
\end{equation}
\indent The local feature describes the decoder output near $i$, the mask token
conditions the update on the decoded instance, and the coordinate and logit
terms specify the position and current decision state. A lightweight MLP $g$
then predicts a bounded residual for each selected location:
\begin{equation}
\delta_i=2\tanh g(\phi_i),\qquad i\in\Omega_r,
\label{eq:udpr}
\end{equation}
\indent The refined logit map is formed by scattering these residuals back to their
original positions:
\begin{equation}
Y^1_{r,i}=\begin{cases}
Y^0_{r,i}+\delta_i, & i\in\Omega_r,\\
Y^0_{r,i}, & i\notin\Omega_r.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:udpr-scatter}
\end{equation}
\indent This selective update leaves all nonselected decoder logits unchanged and
applies bounded residual corrections on the existing SAM2 decoder grid.

\section{EXPERIMENTS}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Experiments use the WHU dataset~\cite{whu}, a single-class building benchmark
with 2,943/627/2,220 training/validation/test images, and the NWPU VHR-10
dataset~\cite{nwpu}, a ten-class benchmark. Because the latter provides
detection annotations only, we use the publicly released instance masks of
Su \textit{et al.}~\cite{nwpu_instance} and its 520/130
train/evaluation split. 

Our PyTorch implementation uses distributed data parallelism on four NVIDIA
RTX 3090 GPUs, with one image per GPU and two-step gradient accumulation
(effective batch size eight). Both benchmark runs initialize from SAM2
Hiera-Base+ weights: base Hiera parameters remain frozen, while its LoRA
adapters and the mask decoder are updated. We use AdamW with an initial
learning rate of $5\times10^{-4}$ and cosine annealing; training uses
cross-entropy, Smooth L1, binary cross-entropy, and Dice losses. A value of
$K=64$ is selected by screening $K\in\{32,64,128\}$ under a common protocol; it achieves
the highest overall bbox AP and mask AP in Table~\ref{tab:k-sensitivity} and
is used in the main experiments.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\vspace{-0.35em}
The effects of adding prompt types and decoder-tail refinement
are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. With only the box
prompt $B$, mask AP is increased from 63.15\% to 65.25\%, and mask AP$_{75}$
from 67.73\% to 69.02\%. With both discrete prompts ($P{+}B$), 66.94\% mask
AP and 72.73\% mask AP$_{75}$ are obtained, showing complementary cues.

With the dense-mask prompt, 66.35\% mask AP and 71.61\% mask AP$_{75}$ are
obtained, while bbox AP and bbox AP$_{75}$ are 73.76\% and 84.69\%,
respectively. For the complete $P{+}B{+}M{+}\mathrm{UDPR}$ configuration,
68.28\% mask AP and 74.61\% mask AP$_{75}$ are obtained, the best mask
results in this ablation.

\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper presents PromptMiner-SAM2, a framework that addresses ambiguous
instance separation in overhead imagery by converting proposal-local shape cues
into complementary prompts for SAM2 and refining uncertain mask locations. The
resulting design improves remote-sensing instance segmentation, reaching 73.4\%
mask AP on the WHU dataset and 68.3\% on the NWPU VHR-10 dataset. Future work
can explore richer prompt-mining schemes, including cues that adapt to object
scale and local scene context.
